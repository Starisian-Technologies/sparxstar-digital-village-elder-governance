## 1. Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose

1.1.1 The Digital Village Elder (DVE) is the AIWA-specific governance-integrated operational stack developed and operated by Starisian Technologies. DVE constitutes the technical environment in which AI West Africa (AIWA) governance policies, contributor permissions, cultural restrictions, provenance requirements, and AI dataset controls are implemented and enforced.

1.1.2 This document — DVE-FRM-01 — is the apex governance document for all DVE-series documents. All DVE standards, controls, procedures, and registries are subordinate to this framework. No DVE-series document may contradict, supersede, or create exceptions to the provisions of this framework without joint amendment by AIWA and Starisian Technologies.

1.1.3 The authoritative one-sentence statement of the DVE architecture is:

> Starisian Technologies operates the DVE infrastructure environment configured to enforce applicable AIWA governance policies and contributor permissions.

This statement governs the interpretation of all DVE documentation. Any provision of a DVE-series document that cannot be reconciled with this statement is void.

### 1.2 Scope

1.2.1 This framework governs:

(a) The operational parameters of the DVE environment;
(b) The relationship between AIWA governance authority and Starisian infrastructure authority within the DVE;
(c) The governance pipeline through which contributor works, folklore, and traditional cultural expressions (TCEs) are processed, classified, and controlled;
(d) The AI processing governance controls applicable to governed artifacts within the DVE;
(e) The cultural restriction enforcement mechanisms binding on the DVE processing pipeline;
(f) The contributor permission model, including consent, revocation, and deletion handling;
(g) The auditability and traceability requirements applicable to all governed artifacts;
(h) The governance interoperability requirements enabling future non-AIWA deployments of the DVE governance model.

1.2.2 This framework does not govern:

(a) The internal corporate governance of AIWA;
(b) The internal corporate governance of Starisian Technologies;
(c) The design, architecture, or ownership of the SPARXSTAR platform;
(d) The specific technical implementation details of Starisian infrastructure, which remain within Starisian's exclusive technical authority.

## 2. Institutional Architecture

### 2.1 The Four-Layer Model

2.1.1 The DVE governance architecture recognises four distinct institutional layers, each with exclusive authority within its domain:

| Layer | Institution | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Governance Authority | AI West Africa (AIWA) | Governance, stewardship, contributor rights, cultural governance, dataset permission authority |
| Infrastructure Authority | Starisian Technologies | Software, infrastructure, AI systems, patents, platform operations |
| Operational Enforcement | Digital Village Elder (DVE) | Governance-integrated operational stack — the bridge between AIWA policy and Starisian infrastructure |
| Platform Foundation | SPARXSTAR | Core platform and infrastructure layer on which DVE runs |

2.1.2 These four layers are institutionally and legally distinct. No layer subsumes, owns, or controls another layer's authority domain. Documents within this repository must not conflate or collapse these layers.

### 2.2 AI West Africa (AIWA)

2.2.1 AIWA is a Gambian-led institution operating under the laws of the Republic of The Gambia. AIWA is the governance and stewardship authority for:

(a) Contributor rights and rights administration;
(b) Cultural stewardship of works submitted through authorised intake channels;
(c) Protection of folklore and Traditional Cultural Expressions (TCEs);
(d) AI dataset governance — including permission, restriction, and revocation of dataset use;
(e) Provenance and consent requirements applicable to all governed artifacts.

2.2.2 AIWA does not own, operate, or direct the SPARXSTAR platform, Starisian infrastructure, or the DVE technical implementation. AIWA's authority is governance authority — the authority to define what is permitted, what is restricted, and what is prohibited within the DVE ecosystem.

2.2.3 AIWA governance documents — including those in the AIWA-CONST and AIWA-POL series — constitute the authoritative governance policy corpus from which DVE operational rules are derived.

### 2.3 Starisian Technologies

2.3.1 Starisian Technologies is an independent company incorporated in California. Starisian is the developer and operator of the SPARXSTAR platform and the DVE operational stack. Starisian holds the technical authority for:

(a) SPARXSTAR platform design, architecture, and operation;
(b) DVE infrastructure design and implementation;
(c) Software development governance and engineering standards;
(d) AI systems design and AI processing infrastructure;
(e) Platform security, resilience, and operational continuity;
(f) Patent and intellectual property protection of Starisian's technology assets.

2.3.2 Starisian Technologies does not hold governance authority over AIWA's classification decisions, contributor rights determinations, or cultural protection designations. Starisian's authority is infrastructure authority — the authority to determine how approved governance operations are implemented safely and reliably.

2.3.3 Starisian's infrastructure policies — documented in the STAR-POL series — establish the engineering and operational constraints within which AIWA governance rules are implemented.

### 2.4 Digital Village Elder (DVE)

2.4.1 DVE is the governance-integrated operational stack developed and operated by Starisian Technologies, configured to enforce applicable AIWA governance policies and contributor permissions. DVE operates at the intersection of AIWA governance authority and Starisian infrastructure authority.

2.4.2 DVE does not possess independent governance authority. DVE enforces governance rules defined by AIWA. DVE does not possess independent infrastructure design authority. DVE operates within infrastructure boundaries established by Starisian.

2.4.3 DVE's operational authority is bounded by:

(a) AIWA governance policies — which define what DVE must enforce;
(b) Starisian infrastructure policies — which define how DVE may implement enforcement;
(c) This framework — which defines the relationship between those two constraint sets.

### 2.5 SPARXSTAR

2.5.1 SPARXSTAR is the core platform and infrastructure layer developed and operated by Starisian Technologies on which DVE runs. SPARXSTAR provides the foundational computational, storage, networking, and services infrastructure on which DVE operational components are deployed.

2.5.2 SPARXSTAR is not a governance entity. SPARXSTAR does not hold governance authority over AIWA's decisions, nor does SPARXSTAR exercise independent authority over contributor works or cultural materials.

## 3. Policy Inheritance from AIWA Governance

### 3.1 The Policy Inheritance Principle

3.1.1 All DVE governance rules derive their authority from AIWA governance documents. DVE does not possess original governance authority. DVE implements and enforces governance authority delegated through the AIWA policy corpus.

3.1.2 The AIWA policy corpus — comprising AIWA-CONST, AIWA-POL, and AIWA-GOV documents — constitutes the source of governance truth for the DVE environment. Where a DVE document is silent on a governance question, the applicable AIWA policy document governs.

3.1.3 Where a DVE operational rule conflicts with an AIWA governance requirement, the AIWA governance requirement prevails. Starisian Technologies is obligated to update DVE operational rules to align with AIWA governance requirements within the timeframes established by the applicable AIWA policy.

### 3.2 Binding AIWA Governance Requirements

3.2.1 The following AIWA governance requirements are binding on the DVE environment:

(a) **Contributor consent requirements** — No governed artifact may enter the DVE processing pipeline without a valid consent record. The form and content of consent is defined by AIWA policy.

(b) **Cultural restriction designations** — AIWA may designate any work, folklore, or TCE as subject to cultural restriction. Cultural restriction designations are binding on the DVE processing pipeline immediately upon designation.

(c) **Dataset use permissions** — AIWA defines the scope of permitted AI dataset use for each governed artifact. DVE processing controls must enforce AIWA dataset use permissions without modification.

(d) **Revocation instructions** — AIWA may issue revocation instructions at any time. DVE is required to implement revocation instructions within the timeframes defined in DVE-PROC (Revocation and Deletion Procedures).

(e) **Provenance requirements** — AIWA establishes the provenance documentation standards applicable to all governed artifacts. DVE must generate, maintain, and preserve provenance records in conformance with AIWA standards.

## 4. Technical Dependence on Starisian Infrastructure Policies

### 4.1 The Infrastructure Dependence Principle

4.1.1 DVE's operational capabilities are bounded by the technical infrastructure maintained by Starisian Technologies. Starisian's infrastructure policies — documented in the STAR-POL series — establish the operational constraints within which DVE governance enforcement is implemented.

4.1.2 DVE does not require Starisian to implement infrastructure capabilities beyond those established in the applicable STAR-POL documents. Where AIWA governance requirements cannot be implemented within existing Starisian infrastructure capabilities, AIWA and Starisian must negotiate infrastructure enhancement through the governance interoperability process defined in Section 12.

4.1.3 Starisian's infrastructure policies establish binding constraints on:

(a) Data retention and deletion capabilities;
(b) Cryptographic signing and verification capabilities;

### 4.2 Binding Starisian Infrastructure Requirements

## 5. Governance Binding Mechanisms

### 5.1 The ArtifactGovernanceDeclaration

5.1.1 Every governed artifact within the DVE carries a permanent, immutable ArtifactGovernanceDeclaration. The ArtifactGovernanceDeclaration constitutes the binding governance record attached to the artifact at the time of intake classification.

5.1.2 The ArtifactGovernanceDeclaration records:

(a) The artifact identifier and classification;
(b) The contributor identity reference (not personal data in cleartext);
(c) The AIWA governance policies applicable to the artifact at the time of classification;
(d) The cultural restriction designations applicable to the artifact;
(e) The dataset use permissions granted by AIWA;
(f) The provenance chain for the artifact;
(g) The timestamp and governance version at which classification occurred.

5.1.3 The ArtifactGovernanceDeclaration is subject to Patent Family C (Multi-Tiered Executable Governance). Its technical implementation details are not documented in governance documents.

### 5.2 The Sieve (Mḗh₁n̥s Governance Enforcement Gate)

5.2.1 The Sieve is the DVE governance enforcement gate through which all payloads must pass before processing. The Sieve evaluates each payload against applicable AIWA governance policies, contributor permissions, and cultural restriction designations.

5.2.2 A payload that fails governance evaluation at the Sieve is placed in QUARANTINE. QUARANTINE is a DVE holding state — it is not deletion. Payloads in QUARANTINE are retained pending governance resolution. The specific QUARANTINE handling procedures are defined in DVE-PROC (QUARANTINE Management Procedures).

5.2.3 A payload that clears governance evaluation at the Sieve receives a Release Receipt. The Release Receipt is the GovernanceToken issued by Mḗh₁n̥s confirming that the payload has cleared the Sieve and is authorised for the requested processing operation.

5.2.4 No processing operation within the DVE may proceed without a valid Release Receipt for the applicable payload and processing operation. This is an absolute requirement. No override mechanism exists at the DVE layer.

### 5.3 Personal Policy Token and Group Policy

5.3.1 The Personal Policy Token is the short-lived contributor governance preference instrument minted by Sky Esu at the time a contributor releases a work. The Personal Policy Token records the contributor's current governance preferences for their work.

5.3.2 Group Policy is the standing community governance authority declaration applicable to works submitted on behalf of a community, community group, or cultural group. Group Policy is senior to Personal Policy.

5.3.3 Where a Group Policy is applicable, DVE must enforce Group Policy requirements regardless of any conflicting Personal Policy Token. The Group Policy hierarchy is defined in AIWA-POL (Contributor Rights and Consent Policy).

## 6. Operational Authority Boundaries

### 6.1 The Three-Sentence Authority Model

6.1.1 The total operational authority model for the DVE ecosystem is expressed in three sentences:

> **AIWA decides** whether a use is authorised.
> **Starisian decides** how approved processing is implemented safely and reliably.
> **DVE enforces** AIWA governance rules through Starisian infrastructure.

6.1.2 These three sentences are not merely descriptive. They are binding authority boundaries. No DVE document, operational procedure, or system configuration may produce an outcome inconsistent with this authority model.

### 6.2 AIWA Authority Boundaries

6.2.1 AIWA's authority within the DVE ecosystem is:

(a) To define which uses of governed artifacts are authorised, restricted, or prohibited;
(b) To designate works, folklore, and TCEs as subject to cultural restriction;
(c) To set contributor consent requirements and consent standards;
(d) To issue revocation instructions for any governed artifact;
(e) To establish the provenance documentation standards binding on the DVE;
(f) To define the governance policies that DVE is required to enforce.

6.2.2 AIWA's authority does not extend to:

(a) The technical implementation details of Starisian infrastructure;
(b) The design or architecture of the SPARXSTAR platform;
(c) Starisian's engineering processes or software development practices;
(d) Starisian's patent and intellectual property strategy.

### 6.3 Starisian Authority Boundaries

6.3.1 Starisian's authority within the DVE ecosystem is:

(a) To determine how approved processing operations are implemented within DVE infrastructure;
(b) To establish the engineering and security standards applicable to DVE operational components;
(c) To maintain and evolve the SPARXSTAR platform and DVE infrastructure;
(d) To hold and protect patents on DVE and SPARXSTAR technical innovations;
(e) To establish the infrastructure constraints within which AIWA governance rules are implemented.

6.3.2 Starisian's authority does not extend to:

(a) Determining which uses of governed artifacts are authorised (that is AIWA's authority);
(b) Overriding or modifying AIWA cultural restriction designations;
(c) Granting or revoking contributor consent on AIWA's behalf;
(d) Issuing governance classification decisions for contributor works.

### 6.4 DVE Operational Authority

6.4.1 DVE's operational authority is enforcement authority only. DVE enforces governance rules defined by AIWA through infrastructure maintained by Starisian. DVE does not originate governance rules and does not make infrastructure design decisions.

6.4.2 DVE operational staff and systems may:

(a) Enforce AIWA governance rules through the Sieve and related mechanisms;
(b) Implement AIWA revocation instructions within defined timeframes;
(c) Generate and maintain provenance records in conformance with AIWA standards;
(d) Operate QUARANTINE management procedures for payloads failing governance evaluation;
(e) Report governance violations to AIWA.

6.4.3 DVE operational staff and systems may not:

(a) Interpret, modify, or waive AIWA governance classifications;
(b) Override a Sieve enforcement decision without a valid Release Receipt;
(c) Approve AI dataset use beyond the permissions granted by AIWA;
(d) Delete a governed artifact without an AIWA revocation instruction or an applicable DVE-PROC procedure authorising deletion.

## 7. Dataset Governance Architecture

### 7.1 The Dataset Governance Principle

7.1.1 All AI dataset use of governed artifacts within the DVE is governed by AIWA's AI dataset governance policy. No governed artifact may be used as AI training data, AI evaluation data, or AI inference data without an explicit AIWA dataset use permission applicable to that artifact.

7.1.2 Dataset use permissions are recorded in the ArtifactGovernanceDeclaration and enforced through DVE processing controls. Dataset use permissions are specific — they define the permitted use, the permitted processing entity, and any applicable temporal or scope constraints.

### 7.2 Dataset Classification Controls

7.2.1 Governed artifacts are classified into the following dataset governance tiers:

(a) **Tier 1 — Unrestricted**: Artifacts authorised for AI training, evaluation, and inference without additional per-use governance checks.

(b) **Tier 2 — Restricted Use**: Artifacts authorised for specific AI uses, subject to per-use governance check through the Sieve.

(c) **Tier 3 — Culturally Restricted**: Artifacts subject to cultural restriction designations. Use in AI datasets requires explicit AIWA authorisation for each dataset use context. TCEs and community-designated folklore fall into this tier by default.

(d) **Tier 4 — Prohibited**: Artifacts for which AIWA has prohibited all AI dataset use. No processing pathway within DVE may include Tier 4 artifacts in any AI dataset operation.

(e) **Tier 5 — QUARANTINE**: Artifacts held pending governance resolution. No AI dataset use is permitted for artifacts in QUARANTINE.

7.2.2 Dataset classification tier is recorded in the ArtifactGovernanceDeclaration. Changes to dataset classification require an AIWA governance decision and generate a governance event in the audit log.

### 7.3 Dataset Pipeline Governance

7.3.1 Every dataset assembly operation within DVE must:

(a) Verify the dataset classification tier of each artifact proposed for inclusion;
(b) Obtain Release Receipts from the Sieve for all Tier 2 artifacts proposed for inclusion;
(c) Obtain explicit AIWA authorisation for all Tier 3 artifacts proposed for inclusion;
(d) Reject inclusion of Tier 4 and Tier 5 artifacts;
(e) Record the dataset composition and governance basis in the audit log.

7.3.2 Dataset assembly operations must not proceed until all applicable Release Receipts are obtained and recorded. Partial datasets composed only of authorised artifacts may proceed with Release Receipts for the authorised subset, provided the Tier 4 and Tier 5 exclusion requirement is satisfied.

## 8. AI Processing Governance

### 8.1 The AI Processing Governance Principle

8.1.1 All AI processing of governed artifacts within DVE is subject to governance evaluation before processing commences. AI processing operations include training, fine-tuning, evaluation, inference generation, embedding, and any other computational operation that processes a governed artifact as input to an AI model.

8.1.2 Governance evaluation for AI processing is performed by the Sieve. AI processing operations that receive a Release Receipt from the Sieve are authorised to proceed. AI processing operations that do not receive a Release Receipt must not proceed.

### 8.2 AI Processing Controls

8.2.1 DVE AI processing controls enforce the following requirements:

(a) **Isolation**: AI processing of governed artifacts must occur within Starisian's designated AI processing environments. Processing must not occur outside the DVE governance boundary.

(b) **Traceability**: AI processing operations must be traceable to the governed artifacts processed, the processing operation performed, the Release Receipt authorising the operation, and the output generated.

(c) **Output governance**: Outputs generated from AI processing of governed artifacts are themselves governed artifacts for the purpose of provenance tracking. The ArtifactGovernanceDeclaration of the source artifacts must be propagated to the output's provenance record.

(d) **No unauthorised derivation**: AI models trained on governed artifacts may not be distributed, deployed, or used outside the DVE governance boundary without AIWA authorisation.

### 8.3 AI Model Governance

8.3.1 AI models trained, fine-tuned, or otherwise derived from governed artifacts within DVE are subject to the following governance requirements:

(a) The AI model is a governed artifact for the purpose of this framework;
(b) The AI model's ArtifactGovernanceDeclaration must reference the provenance of the training dataset and the Release Receipts authorising dataset use;
(c) The AI model's deployment and use are subject to AIWA authorisation;
(d) Revocation of dataset use permissions that were relied upon in training an AI model requires AIWA to determine the applicable remedy, which may include model retirement, targeted removal, or restricted deployment.

## 9. Cultural Restriction Enforcement

### 9.1 Cultural Restriction Designations

9.1.1 AIWA may designate any work, folklore, or TCE as subject to cultural restriction. Cultural restriction designations are AIWA governance decisions. DVE enforces cultural restriction designations as received — DVE does not interpret, modify, or waive cultural restriction designations.

9.1.2 The following categories of material are subject to cultural restriction by default, absent an AIWA determination to the contrary:

(a) Folklore — expressions of community cultural heritage with no identifiable individual author, transmitted through generations;
(b) TCEs — Traditional Cultural Expressions protected under Gambian law and applicable international frameworks;
(c) Ceremonial, ritual, and sacred materials designated as such by the originating community;
(d) Materials designated as subject to community governance authority by a Group Policy declaration.

### 9.2 Enforcement Mechanisms

9.2.1 Cultural restriction designations are enforced through the following DVE mechanisms:

(a) **Classification tagging**: All governed artifacts carry cultural restriction status in their ArtifactGovernanceDeclaration. Cultural restriction tags are propagated to all derivative artifacts and dataset compositions that include the restricted artifact.

(b) **Sieve enforcement**: The Sieve evaluates cultural restriction status as a primary governance criterion. No processing operation that would violate a cultural restriction designation may receive a Release Receipt.

(c) **Dataset exclusion**: Culturally restricted artifacts are assigned Tier 3 or higher dataset classification. Tier 3 artifacts require explicit AIWA authorisation for each dataset use context.

(d) **Distribution controls**: Culturally restricted artifacts may not be distributed outside the DVE governance boundary without explicit AIWA authorisation for the specific distribution context.

### 9.3 Community Governance Authority

9.3.1 Community governance authority — expressed through Group Policy declarations — is senior to individual contributor governance preferences. DVE enforces community governance authority over individual contributor preferences where Group Policy is applicable.

9.3.2 Group Policy declarations are issued by AIWA on behalf of communities that have established governance authority with AIWA. DVE enforces Group Policy declarations as received from AIWA.

## 10. Contributor Permission and Revocation Handling

### 10.1 Contributor Permissions

10.1.1 A contributor is a person who submits a work to AIWA through an authorised intake channel. Contributor permissions govern the scope of permitted use of the contributor's work within the DVE.

10.1.2 Contributor permissions are recorded in the contributor's Personal Policy Token and in the ArtifactGovernanceDeclaration of submitted works. Contributor permissions are:

(a) Specific — they define the scope of permitted use;
(b) Temporal — they may carry expiry conditions;
(c) Revocable — contributors may revoke permissions subject to AIWA's revocation policy;
(d) Hierarchically subordinate to Group Policy where applicable.

### 10.2 Consent Requirements

10.2.1 Contributor consent is required for all intake operations. Consent must be:

(a) Informed — the contributor must understand what they are consenting to;
(b) Voluntary — consent must not be induced through coercion, false representation, or material omission;
(c) Specific — consent must be given for specific uses, not blanket consent for all uses;
(d) Recorded — consent must be recorded in the ArtifactGovernanceDeclaration at the time of intake.

10.2.2 AIWA's consent standards, defined in AIWA-POL (Contributor Rights and Consent Policy), are binding on the DVE intake process.

### 10.3 Revocation Handling

10.3.1 A contributor may request revocation of permissions through AIWA's authorised revocation channel. AIWA processes revocation requests and issues revocation instructions to DVE.

10.3.2 Upon receipt of an AIWA revocation instruction, DVE must:

(a) Immediately prevent further processing of the affected artifact under the revoked permission;
(b) Update the ArtifactGovernanceDeclaration to record the revocation and its effective date;
(c) Identify all dataset compositions, AI processing operations, and derivative artifacts that relied upon the revoked permission;
(d) Report the impact assessment to AIWA within the timeframe defined in DVE-PROC (Revocation Impact Assessment Procedures);
(e) Implement the disposition determined by AIWA for each affected downstream artifact.

10.3.3 Revocation does not automatically require deletion of all downstream artifacts. Deletion is an AIWA governance decision. DVE implements deletion instructions as received. Pending AIWA's deletion decision, affected downstream artifacts are placed in QUARANTINE.

### 10.4 Deletion Handling

10.4.1 AIWA may issue a deletion instruction for a governed artifact. Upon receipt of a deletion instruction, DVE must:

(a) Delete the specified artifact from the active DVE environment;
(b) Delete all copies of the specified artifact within the DVE governance boundary, except where retention is required by applicable law or by AIWA governance policy;
(c) Record the deletion event in the audit log, including the deletion instruction reference and the confirmation of deletion;
(d) Report deletion completion to AIWA within the timeframe defined in DVE-PROC (Deletion Handling Procedures).

10.4.2 Deletion of a governed artifact does not delete the ArtifactGovernanceDeclaration record associated with that artifact. ArtifactGovernanceDeclaration records are retained as permanent governance records.

## 11. Auditability and Traceability

### 11.1 The Auditability Principle

11.1.1 All governance-relevant events within the DVE are recorded in an append-only audit log. The audit log constitutes the authoritative record of DVE governance operations.

11.1.2 The audit log is implemented within Starisian's audit infrastructure. The audit log must be:

(a) Append-only — no audit log entry may be modified or deleted after writing;
(b) Tamper-evident — audit log integrity must be verifiable by AIWA and by Starisian;
(c) Complete — all governance-relevant events must be logged;
(d) Retained — audit log entries must be retained for the period defined in AIWA-POL (Audit Retention Policy).

### 11.2 Governance-Relevant Events

11.2.1 The following events are governance-relevant and must be recorded in the audit log:

(a) Artifact intake and initial classification;
(b) ArtifactGovernanceDeclaration creation and all subsequent amendments;
(c) Sieve evaluation decisions (pass and fail);
(d) Release Receipt issuance;
(e) QUARANTINE placement and release;
(f) Dataset assembly operations and their governance basis;
(g) AI processing operations and their Release Receipts;
(h) Cultural restriction designation changes;
(i) Contributor permission updates and Personal Policy Token minting;
(j) Group Policy declarations and amendments;
(k) Revocation instruction receipt and implementation;
(l) Deletion instruction receipt and implementation;
(m) All governance escalations to AIWA.

### 11.3 Traceability Requirements

11.3.1 The DVE must provide traceability from any output artifact back to the governed artifacts that contributed to its creation. Traceability is maintained through:

(a) The ArtifactGovernanceDeclaration provenance chain;
(b) The Release Receipt chain for each processing operation in the provenance chain;
(c) The audit log entries for each governance-relevant event in the artifact's lifecycle.

11.3.2 AIWA has the right to request a complete traceability report for any governed artifact at any time. DVE must produce traceability reports within the timeframe defined in DVE-PROC (Traceability Report Procedures).

## 12. Governance Interoperability

### 12.1 The Interoperability Principle

12.1.1 The DVE governance model is designed to support governance interoperability — the capacity to extend the DVE governance architecture to non-AIWA institutional deployments, while preserving the structural separation of governance authority, infrastructure authority, and enforcement operations.

12.1.2 Governance interoperability does not authorise any non-AIWA institution to exercise AIWA's governance authority. Non-AIWA institutional deployments of the DVE model operate under their own governance authority, within an equivalent structural model.

### 12.2 Interoperability Requirements

12.2.1 A DVE governance model deployment for a non-AIWA institution must:

(a) Maintain the three-layer authority model: a governance authority, an infrastructure authority, and an enforcement operational stack;
(b) Implement equivalent ArtifactGovernanceDeclaration, Sieve, and Release Receipt mechanisms;
(c) Implement equivalent contributor permission, cultural restriction, and revocation handling mechanisms;
(d) Implement equivalent audit and traceability requirements;
(e) Not represent itself as operating under AIWA governance authority unless AIWA has explicitly authorised such representation.

12.2.2 Governance interoperability is a Starisian Technologies platform capability, subject to Starisian's patent rights and licensing authority. Deployment of the DVE governance model for non-AIWA institutions requires Starisian's authorisation and is governed by STAR-POL (Governance Interoperability Policy).

### 12.3 Future Governance Evolution

12.3.1 This framework is intended to evolve as the DVE ecosystem matures. Amendments to this framework require joint agreement between AIWA and Starisian Technologies.

12.3.2 Amendments that affect AIWA's governance authority require AIWA's affirmative agreement. Amendments that affect Starisian's infrastructure authority require Starisian's affirmative agreement. Amendments that affect only DVE operational procedures may be made by joint agreement of the DVE operational governance committee, subject to ratification by AIWA and Starisian.
