## 1. Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose

1.1.1 This document establishes the legal and jurisdictional framework applicable to AIWA's governance authority, contributor rights administration, cultural protection obligations, and dataset governance operations.

1.1.2 This document provides the legal basis for AIWA's authority over the DVE governance pipeline and identifies the legal frameworks that govern AIWA's obligations to contributors, communities, and the Republic of The Gambia.

### 1.2 Primary Jurisdiction

1.2.1 AIWA is a Gambian-led institution. The primary legal jurisdiction applicable to AIWA's governance authority is the Republic of The Gambia.

1.2.2 Gambian law governs:

(a) AIWA's institutional authority and governance powers;
(b) The protection of Gambian cultural heritage and Traditional Cultural Expressions;
(c) Contributor rights as applicable under Gambian intellectual property law;
(d) AIWA's obligations to communities operating under Gambian law.

### 1.3 Starisian's Legal Jurisdiction

1.3.1 Starisian Technologies is incorporated in California. The primary legal jurisdiction applicable to Starisian's infrastructure authority and patent rights is California and applicable United States federal law.

1.3.2 The legal independence of AIWA and Starisian is a foundational governance principle. Neither AIWA nor Starisian is a subsidiary, agent, or controlled affiliate of the other.

---

## 2. Applicable Legal Frameworks

### 2.1 Gambian Law

2.1.1 The following Gambian legal instruments are applicable to AIWA's governance authority:

(a) The Copyright Act of The Gambia — governing contributor rights in works fixed in tangible form;
(b) Gambian constitutional protections applicable to cultural heritage and community rights;
(c) Data protection legislation applicable to contributor personal data.

### 2.2 International Frameworks

2.2.1 The following international frameworks are recognised as interpretive instruments for AIWA's governance obligations. They are not controlling authority — Gambian law governs:

(a) UNESCO Convention for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage (2003) — reference framework for TCE protection principles;
(b) WIPO Intergovernmental Committee frameworks on genetic resources, traditional knowledge, and traditional cultural expressions — reference framework for TCE governance principles;
(c) UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (UNDRIP) — interpretive reference for community rights principles.

2.2.2 AIWA's cultural governance policies draw upon these international frameworks as interpretive support. No provision of an AIWA governance document may be construed as granting controlling authority to any international body over AIWA's governance decisions.

### 2.3 Cross-Border Considerations

2.3.1 The DVE processes governed artifacts that may originate from communities across West Africa and beyond. AIWA's governance framework is designed to respect the customary and legal rights of originating communities regardless of national jurisdiction.

2.3.2 Where a governed artifact originates from a community governed by laws of a jurisdiction other than The Gambia, AIWA shall apply its governance framework in a manner consistent with the applicable rights of the originating community.

---

## 3. Liability and Indemnity Framework

### 3.1 Institutional Separation

3.1.1 AIWA and Starisian Technologies are independent legal entities. Neither institution accepts liability for the obligations or acts of the other.

3.1.2 AIWA's governance decisions are AIWA's institutional responsibility. Starisian's infrastructure operations are Starisian's institutional responsibility. DVE enforces AIWA governance decisions through Starisian infrastructure — DVE enforcement operations do not create liability between AIWA and Starisian except as expressly agreed in the applicable interoperability agreement.
